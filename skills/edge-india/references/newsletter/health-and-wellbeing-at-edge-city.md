## Your Sankalpa ✨

Sankalpa is a Sanskrit word for an intention or resolve, something you deeply want to cultivate. It’s also the name of our Edge accountability bot.

Before you arrive, choose yours, the one thing you’d like to be different by the time you leave Goa. It could be meditating each morning, moving every day, sleeping more, or spending less time on your phone.

Tell Sankalpa, and it will gently remind you when the village inevitably gets busy. Nobody’s keeping score, and it’s simply there to support your own flourishing.

## Default Healthy

Wellness runs right through the village calendar, so there’s always something close by.

-   **Yoga and movement**, including yoga classes, workouts, walks, and swims, with plenty of space to roll out your own mat

-   **Breathwork and meditation** to settle your breath, attention, and nervous system

-   **Ecstatic dance**, for an hour out of your head and in your body

-   **Sound baths and recovery** for the days you’d rather do nothing at all

-   **Ayurveda**, including time with an Ayurvedic practitioner

-   **Connection**, through community dinners, circles, and a few hundred people living side by side

Edge gets busy, and there will always be another talk, dinner, or person you feel you have to meet. You won’t want to do all of it, and that’s completely fine. Find what makes you feel good, take an afternoon off when you need one, and every so often check back in with your Sankalpa.

## What to bring ☀️

Goa is hot, humid, and sunny in October, so pack with that in mind.

-   **Your own yoga mat**

-   A hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen

-   Mosquito repellent

-   Light, breathable clothes, plus something that covers your shoulders and knees for sacred spaces

-   A reusable water bottle and some electrolytes

-   Comfortable shoes and sandals

-   A light rain layer

-   Your usual medications and supplements, and a small first-aid kit

Important note: Please also make sure your travel insurance covers medical care before you fly.

## Being good guests 🇮🇳

India has a deep healing tradition, and some of the practices we’ll share have been refined here over thousands of years. We’re guests, so come curious, ask where things come from, and treat sacred spaces and local customs with care.

The same care goes for each other. Consent should always be clear, enthusiastic, informed, and ongoing.

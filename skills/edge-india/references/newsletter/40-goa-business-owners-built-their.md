Anyone can run an AI workshop. We ran ours inside Edge City, alongside a few hundred people building at the frontier of technology, and 40 local business owners in Goa built their own AI tools in three weeks. Leaders who want this for their community can reach out to [Mariella Torres](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mariella-torres/) or [Noah White](https://www.linkedin.com/in/noahcwhite/) to talk about taking part in a future Edge City.

Governments everywhere are funding AI skills for small businesses. The question now is what actually works for a shop owner on a Tuesday afternoon. If AI is going to make life better for most people, it has to work for local businesses first, because a community does well when its local economy does. In Goa, what worked was owners building their own tools, each one made for their own business, usually for a fraction of the cost of off-the-shelf software and with no monthly subscription. They worked with one technical builder for every six businesses, at no cost to the owners. Most owners chose marketing as their biggest opportunity, and many also built tools for daily operations like inventory, purchasing and staff shifts.

Edge City is where people come to build new technology together. They prototype, get answers fast, and use each answer to run the next experiment. Edge City brings together a few hundred builders, researchers, artists and families for several weeks at a time to take on ambitious projects. In October, it came to Goa, India. Ground Floor ran as a residency in partnership with Edge City, and we designed the program ourselves. We hosted free AI build sessions for local business owners every Tuesday and Thursday afternoon. In three weeks, 60 businesses signed up and 40 owners built their own tools, most with little or no coding experience.

By the final showcase, a jewelry store was running an inventory system on its shop phone, and a recruitment firm that manages 20,000 candidates had a WhatsApp chatbot. A pop-up restaurant was using a purchasing calculator to order the right amount for every event, and a six-week-old coffee shop took orders from AI agents and had its best day of sales to date.

We moved that fast because we ran the sessions inside Edge City. What those owners built also shows where AI helps everyday businesses most, which is exactly what leaders need to know as they plan for what’s next. Here’s what we did, why it worked, and why community builders, governments and anyone working with emerging technology belong at Edge City.

### **Why it worked**

**Edge City started this work months before we arrived.** Telamon first scouted Goa as a location, and he and Dawn from the Edge City team spent months meeting people, hosting dinners and building relationships across the state. Many of the local partners in this story, including Mayur Sontakke of [NomadGao](https://nomadgao.com/), first met Edge through those conversations.

**The culture matters just as much.** People at Edge City start projects without waiting for permission, and they join each other’s work freely. The kids built a many-headed effigy out of cardboard, and each head was hollow. Throughout the day, people wrote something they were ready to let go of on a sticky note and stuffed it inside. At community dinner that night, we burned it in a bonfire. Everyone there leads their own work, and nobody leads alone.

**Locals noticed the difference.** In week two, Shivansh, the owner of [India Yoga School](https://indiayogaschool.com/), located near the venue, joined our sessions. He told us his hometown felt different and he wanted to know why, so he went looking. He found Edge City, then found Ground Floor, and decided that was where he could take part.

### **Three moves that made it work**

Emergence architecture comes down to three moves, and almost everything that follows came from them. Edge City makes room for all three, because residencies are free to change course as they learn.

**Open many conversations.** Teams that want local businesses to adopt AI need to be there in person. Meeting in person turned local partners’ interest into specific commitments within days. Mayur Sontakke of NomadGao connected us to the [Goa Chamber of Commerce and Industry](https://www.goachamber.org/), the state’s leading business association, and we held a joint press conference with them. After the press conference, DS Prashant, CEO of the [Goa Startup Mission](https://startup.goa.gov.in/), the state government’s startup agency, personally championed the program and dedicated his time to getting businesses in his network signed up.

**Notice.** The Government of Goa launched its AI Policy 2026 the same month. The policy includes a proposed innovation fund of ₹250 crore (about $28 million), free AI-readiness assessments for up to 600 small and mid-sized businesses, and a goal of certifying 50,000 people in AI by 2030. Reaching those goals depends on businesses building with AI, which was already happening in the sessions.

On October 22, Noah and I spoke on the closing panel of the state’s AI conference and shared the results, with 60 businesses signed up and 40 owners building their own tools in three weeks. A focused program like this gives a policy like Goa’s a working model for running the sessions, evidence of where AI helps local businesses most, and local partners ready to continue.

### **How the sessions worked**

Every Tuesday and Thursday, 2 to 4 pm, business owners came to build at Riva Beach Resort, Edge City’s main venue. Each one got Claude Max, Wispr Flow and plenty of one-on-one time with our residents, technical builders who sat beside them as they worked. Some owners traveled 90 minutes to be there.

**Week 1. Get set up.** Owners got onboarded, built out context about their businesses, got comfortable with the tools, and named one task that takes up too much of their time.

**Week 2. Find the leverage.** Each owner focused on their highest-leverage opportunity across operations, sales or marketing. Most chose marketing.

**Week 3. Build and demo.** Owners kept building right up to the last moment, then presented what they made at the final showcase.

**Owners who had never written code left trusting themselves as builders.** As one put it at the showcase, “I just built it in one day.”

### **What it took**

> **Business owners** who are curious and ready to build\
> **Technical builders**, about one for every six businesses\
> **Local partners** across community, business and government\
> **Tech partners** who give businesses free access to AI tools\
> **A venue** with a screen, reliable Wi-Fi and power\
> **Enough time** for owners to come back week after week and build confidence

Edge City provided the venue, a few hundred builders to draw from and the excitement of a place where people try new things every day. Business owners could join any of the experimental work happening around them, across technology, science and culture. It also put local businesses in front of decision makers. We introduced Goa’s Minister of Tourism and IT to For Kicks during his visit, and DS Prashant of the Goa Startup Mission was in the audience at the final showcase.

### **What they built**

**A jewelry store** had staff counting inventory on paper every day. The owner built an app where staff log counts by section with photos, plus a dashboard that flags mismatches and exports reports. He built it in one day, and it was on the shop phone the next morning. Next, he’s automating sales data for tax reporting.

**A pop-up restaurant** kept over-ordering ingredients out of worry that guests would leave hungry. The owner built a tool that calculates quantities from RSVPs and price point, then sends a shopping list straight to WhatsApp. Next is ordering from vendors automatically and routing leftovers to donation.

**A tour operator** with 12 years in business was losing bookings as more travelers booked online. He built his own website connecting Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, a step toward taking bookings directly.

Other owners built marketing pipelines, a recruitment chatbot and a travel quiz that drew 32 responses on its first day.

### **A record-breaking day for a brand-new coffee shop**

**New owners, new builders.** For Kicks is a coffee shop in Mandrem, run by the wife-and-husband team Lia and Prateek. It opened a month before we arrived. They were among the first people I met in Goa, and we quickly became close friends. They joined our sessions from the start. Prateek built an app to run the cafe’s shifts, and Lia, who never thought AI was for her, left saying, “I can do this. I can use AI.”

**More customers in a slow season.** Edge City tends to arrive in the shoulder season, when local businesses usually see fewer customers. For three weeks, For Kicks had about 400 residents to sell to and ran pop-ups several times a week, testing new flavors.

**A first for Edge City.** Every resident also had an AI agent in [Agent Village](https://edgecity.notion.site/Research-Partner-Proposal-Agent-Village-V2-at-Edge-City-India-Shared-3c1d45cdfc5981d7b7bac1e216f3678b), and Edge City co-founder Timour wanted locals involved. A new coffee shop, owners eager to experiment and a village full of AI agents meant everything was in place. I chose and brought in For Kicks, Jmill led the technical build, and Lia and Prateek refined the menu until every order worked. Residents ordered coffee through their agents, online and in person. It was For Kicks’ best day of sales to date.

**Why it matters.** We ran it carefully, because For Kicks is a real business with real revenue and employees who depend on it. AI agents buying things is usually tested by large companies. At Edge City, a six-week-old coffee shop in Goa tested it. Agents could help visiting communities find and order from local businesses, help owners plan stock around real demand, and show how much spending stays local. That’s the kind of result leaders need to see.

### **What happens next**

**Goa already had much of what it needs, including talented founders, committed institutions, generous connectors and businesses eager to grow.** The residency gave them a place to meet for three weeks, and that drew in even more people.

**The Goa Chamber of Commerce and Industry is committed to carrying this forward and connecting its members to future sessions.** Two of our residents, Goa locals Shivax, a business consultant, and Yashaswini, a tech and AI lawyer, started the North Goa Tech and AI Community, which already has 73 members on WhatsApp. They run build sessions, Tech & AI for Goa Business clinics where owners bring a real problem, and demo days. They host events regularly and are now scoping upcoming hackathons.

**Every owner in those sessions had a different opportunity, so each one built a different tool.** Many local business needs are specific to one shop, one team or one town. Software built for the mass market often misses them or only comes close. Now owners can build exactly what fits their business, usually for a fraction of the cost and with no monthly subscription. Having run this model in the United States and India, I’ve seen it work best when the program starts with local needs.

_**Leaders preparing their communities for AI need to be futurists who actively shape what comes next, testing ideas early and learning from what local businesses build. Edge City is where I’ve seen that happen fastest.**_

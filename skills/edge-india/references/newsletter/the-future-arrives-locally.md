Charles Darwin boarded the Beagle. Jane Goodall lived at Gombe. When Google wanted to understand the world’s streets, it sent cars covered in cameras down every road because satellites couldn’t see the ground.

Every frontier has required a new way of learning.

This one does too.

Technology is reshaping local economies faster than institutions can learn from them. New tools arrive every week. Industries reorganize. Governments write policy. Small businesses decide what to adopt, what to ignore, and how to preserve what makes them valuable in the first place.

The most important lessons are unfolding inside family businesses, city halls, coffee shops, startups, schools, and neighborhoods.

**Some forms of knowledge can only be gathered in the field.**

That institution should exist.

**We’re building it.**

* * *

# **Goa**

Goa is one of the most interesting places in the world to study technological transformation.

More than 800 startups already call the state home. Roughly one-third are women-led. Nearly a third of the workforce is employed in tourism, much of it through independent family businesses that have shaped the region for generations.

At the same time, Goa has set an ambitious public vision for its future.

Its AI policy is released in September.

Ground Floor arrives in October, when policy begins meeting everyday reality.

That is where the most important learning happens: inside businesses, communities and the relationships that determine whether new technologies become useful, trusted, and locally valuable.

* * *

# **The Residency**

Ground Floor takes place within **[Edge City Goa](https://open.substack.com/pub/edgecityindia2026/p/welcome-to-edge-city-india?r=7jhl3k&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web)**, the first India chapter of the pop-up village that has previously convened in Austin, Patagonia, Cape Town, and Healdsburg.

Over three weeks, we’ll bring together **10 residents** from entrepreneurship, research, economics, public policy, engineering, design, and the arts.

Residents will embed alongside Goan businesses, local governments, researchers, and community organizations, documenting what technological transformation looks like while it’s still unfolding.

Alongside the residency, we’ll convene **100 Goan business owners** through a series of field labs hosted with local governments, chambers of commerce, and community partners.

The businesses bring lived experience.

The residents bring research, technical expertise, and fresh perspectives.

Ground Floor exists to create the conditions where those forms of knowledge meet.

Much of today’s AI tooling struggles to serve the languages many Goans use every day. What would it take to close that gap for Konkani? Who should own the data? Who benefits when the infrastructure is built?

Many more questions will emerge once we’re on the ground. Every technological transition creates new value.

The enduring question is who captures it, who contributes to it, and how communities remain authors of their own future as change accelerates around them.

* * *

# **Healdsburg**

In June, we embedded frontier builders in a small California town for a month.

Together with the Healdsburg Chamber of Commerce, we hosted free AI field labs for local businesses. Residents worked alongside entrepreneurs, explored practical applications, and spent less time talking about the future than building inside it.

We expected enthusiasm.

What we found was discernment.

The business owners who benefited most weren’t the ones who adopted the most tools. They were the ones who became clear about what deserved their attention and what didn’t. Knowing what to ignore turned out to be just as valuable as knowing what to embrace.

Healdsburg gave us our first signal.

Goa is the next chapter.
